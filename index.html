What's included

Built for groups that need real privacy.

Not privacy as a marketing line. Encryption that's architecturally enforced — the server physically cannot read your messages.

End-to-end encrypted channels
Channel messages are encrypted client-side via AES-GCM-256, keyed from your channel passphrase via PBKDF2. The server receives and stores ciphertext. Decryption only happens in your browser, with your key.
AES-GCM-256 · PBKDF2-SHA256
Encrypted direct messages
DMs use ECDH P-256 key exchange — your private key never leaves your device. The server is a blind relay for DMs, delivering ciphertext it cannot read.
ECDH P-256 · HKDF-SHA256
Voice & video calls
Peer-to-peer WebRTC calls — Recline is never in the audio or video path. DTLS-SRTP handles media encryption end-to-end. Screen sharing up to 1440p included.
WebRTC · DTLS-SRTP · P2P
Screen sharing
Share your screen or any application window directly in voice channels. Works on desktop and mobile browsers. Screen share tiles render alongside camera feeds in the call layout.
WebRTC · 1440p
Custom roles & permissions

Security model

The technical specifics.

Privacy claims are easy. Here's what the implementation actually does — and what the server can and can't see.

Server can see
Your username (stored in plain text)
Message timestamps
Which channels you belong to
That a message was sent (not content)
Your IP (not logged by default)
Server cannot see
Message content — encrypted before sending
DM content — ECDH, server is a blind relay
Your channel passphrase
Email or phone — never asked
Call audio or video — WebRTC is P2P

This is alpha software. Key management and metadata minimization are ongoing. Full privacy and security details →
